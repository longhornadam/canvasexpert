RUBRIC: ELA 7 Standard Writing Rubric (100 points)

CRITERION 1: Organization & Structure — 25 points
Core question: Can the reader follow the writer's thinking from start to finish without getting lost?
  [25 pts (band 23-25)] Exemplary: Purposeful introduction hooks the reader and establishes clear context. Body paragraphs follow a logical, intentional sequence that builds meaning. Transitions between and within paragraphs are smooth and purposeful. Conclusion provides closure and extends thinking beyond summary. The overall structure is consistent from start to finish.
  [22 pts (band 19-22)] Proficient: Introduction and conclusion are present and functional. Body paragraphs are organized with a recognizable structure. Transitions are present but may occasionally feel formulaic. The structure generally supports the development of ideas, though some sections may feel disconnected.
  [18 pts (band 13-18)] Developing: An introduction or conclusion may be missing or underdeveloped. Organizational pattern is attempted but inconsistent. Transitions are weak, missing, or repetitive ("also," "another reason"). The reader has to work to follow the writer's line of thinking.
  [12 pts (band 1-12)] Beginning: No clear introduction or conclusion. No recognizable organizational structure — ideas appear random or listed. No transitions. The writing reads as a collection of loosely related sentences rather than a cohesive piece.
  [0 pts] No Score: No response, off-topic, or too brief to evaluate structure.

CRITERION 2: Claim & Focus — 20 points
Core question: Is there a clear, arguable position that the writer maintains without drifting?
  [20 pts (band 18-20)] Exemplary: The controlling idea/claim is clearly stated, specific, and arguable. Every paragraph connects back to the claim with unwavering focus. The writer maintains a consistent position throughout without contradicting or drifting.
  [17 pts (band 15-17)] Proficient: A claim is identifiable and mostly maintained. Focus is generally consistent but may drift slightly in one section. The reader understands the writer's position without confusion.
  [14 pts (band 10-14)] Developing: A claim is present but may be vague, too broad, or buried. Focus shifts noticeably — the writer may argue multiple positions or lose track of the original point. The reader must infer the main idea.
  [9 pts (band 1-9)] Beginning: No identifiable claim, or the claim is a statement of fact rather than an arguable position. Writing lacks focus — it may retell, summarize, or ramble without a clear purpose.
  [0 pts] No Score: No response, off-topic, or too brief to identify a claim.

CRITERION 3: Evidence & Support — 20 points
Core question: Does the writer use specific, relevant text evidence to back up the claim?
  [20 pts (band 18-20)] Exemplary: Evidence is specific, relevant, and well-chosen. Quotes and/or paraphrases are accurately drawn from the text and clearly support the claim. Multiple pieces of evidence are used across body paragraphs. For paired texts, evidence is drawn from both sources.
  [17 pts (band 15-17)] Proficient: Evidence is present and mostly relevant. At least two pieces of text-based evidence are used. Evidence generally supports the claim, though some selections may be less strategically chosen. For paired texts, evidence comes from at least one source.
  [14 pts (band 10-14)] Developing: Evidence is limited, vague, or partially irrelevant. The writer may rely on one piece of evidence or use evidence that only loosely connects to the claim. Quotes may be dropped in without context ("hit-and-run" quoting).
  [9 pts (band 1-9)] Beginning: Little to no text-based evidence. The writer may make unsupported assertions or use personal opinion in place of textual support. Any evidence present is irrelevant or inaccurate.
  [0 pts] No Score: No evidence provided, or response is off-topic/too brief.

CRITERION 4: Commentary & Development — 20 points
Core question: Does the writer explain HOW and WHY the evidence supports the claim, or just drop it in?
  [20 pts (band 18-20)] Exemplary: The writer thoroughly explains how and why each piece of evidence supports the claim. Commentary goes beyond restating — it analyzes, interprets, or connects evidence to the bigger picture. The ratio of commentary to evidence is balanced or commentary-heavy. Ideas are fully developed.
  [17 pts (band 15-17)] Proficient: Commentary is present and generally explains the evidence. The writer connects evidence to the claim but may rely on surface-level explanation ("This shows that…") rather than deep analysis. Development is adequate but not thorough.
  [14 pts (band 10-14)] Developing: Commentary is minimal or formulaic. The writer may quote then immediately move to the next point without explaining. Evidence does most of the work — the writer's own thinking is hard to find. Development is thin.
  [9 pts (band 1-9)] Beginning: No meaningful commentary. The response retells, summarizes, or lists without analysis. Evidence (if present) is never explained. The writer's own thinking is absent.
  [0 pts] No Score: No commentary, off-topic, or too brief to evaluate.

CRITERION 5: Conventions & Language — 15 points
Core question: Can the reader understand the writing without being tripped up by errors?
  [15 pts (band 14-15)] Exemplary: Consistent command of grade-level conventions. Few if any errors in spelling, grammar, punctuation, and capitalization. Sentence variety enhances readability. Word choice is specific and purposeful.
  [13 pts (band 11-13)] Proficient: General command of conventions with some errors that do not impede clarity. Sentences are mostly varied. Word choice is adequate but may occasionally be vague or repetitive.
  [10 pts (band 7-10)] Developing: Several errors in conventions that sometimes impede clarity. Sentence structure is often simple or repetitive. Word choice is general or imprecise.
  [6 pts (band 1-6)] Beginning: Frequent errors significantly interfere with readability. Sentences may be fragments, run-ons, or structurally broken. Word choice is vague or incorrect.
  [0 pts] No Score: Illegible, no response, or errors make the writing incomprehensible.

SCORING PRINCIPLES
- Structure and consistency weighted above surface correctness — a well-organized essay with a few comma errors always outscores a grammatically clean essay that wanders
- Conventions capped at 15% — cannot sink an otherwise strong paper
- Consistency thread runs through ALL categories, not just Organization
- A 0 in any category = no response, off-topic, or too brief to evaluate
- If a student wrote anything on-topic, minimum score is 1 in that category
- Score each category independently — a paper can be Exemplary in one and Beginning in another
- Read once for overall impression, then score on the second pass with the rubric in hand.
- Use anchor papers — calibrate with 2-3 pre-scored exemplars at the start of each scoring session, especially across periods.
- The 0 is reserved for non-attempts. Anything on-topic earns at least a 1 in that category.
- Let the numbers fall where they fall — do not smooth categories toward each other.
For SCRs (1-3 sentences), expectations within each category scale proportionally. A 3-sentence response won't have multiple body paragraphs, but should still show a clear claim, relevant evidence, and at least one sentence of commentary. Score based on whether elements are present and functional at proportional scale.
