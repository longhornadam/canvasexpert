Writing Record - one student's writing history over time

Reference for an AI assistant helping a teacher coach a writer, not just grade
one assignment, in CanvasExpert. Accurate as of this file's version. Answer
from it rather than guessing, and say plainly when a question goes past what
it covers.


What the Writing Record is

Separate from a Canvas course entirely. Some teachers run a daily or weekly
short-writing practice (a "rep") that CanvasExpert scores automatically
against a published checklist and keeps a private, per-student record of over
time: every piece, its score, dated noticings about patterns in the writing,
and specific instructions the teacher gave that student and whether they
followed them. get_writing_history(pseudonym) reads that record. It is
pseudonym-first, not course-first or assignment-first, because the question it
answers is "how is this writer developing," and no other tool can answer that.

This has nothing to do with Writing Timeline. Writing Timeline reads a
submitted Word document's own revision history for one assignment. The
Writing Record is a running history across many short pieces, built by
CanvasExpert's own scoring, not by anything in the document.


What it returns

Dated submissions in ascending order, oldest first. Each one carries its
score and the total possible, the tier it was checked against, how many words
the student actually wrote, and how many dated observations exist for it. Also
the checklist prompt the student was writing to, and the per-criterion
checklist outcome for that piece. Alongside the submissions: any standing
instructions the teacher has given this student and whether the student has
been following them, and the rolling coaching profile CanvasExpert already
keeps for the student: per-criterion rates, active patterns, what to focus on
next, and whether the student looks ready to move up a tier.

There is no trend, streak, or overall judgment baked into the tool's answer.
It hands you dated records; reasoning about the arc, and saying so to the
teacher, is your job as the assistant reading it, the same way a teacher would
read a folder of a student's work over a semester.

include_text defaults to false, which leaves out every span quoted from the
student's own writing: the piece's own text, the exact sentence behind an
observation, the quote behind a checklist outcome. Call with include_text=true
only when you actually need to read the words, and prefer max_text_chars to
trim what comes back rather than pulling everything by default: a long arc of
full text is a large payload, billed to the teacher's own AI key. The
checklist prompt itself is not student data and always comes through, because
without it a piece's purpose is impossible to read: a narrative piece scored
lower than an argument piece the week before is not decline, it is a
different assignment, and the prompt is the only thing here that says so.


What it does not have yet

Only two of the three things a teacher would want are here. The prompt and
scaffolding the student was given: yes. The student's actual submission,
attributed sentence by sentence to where it came from (the assignment, a
scaffold, the student, a quoted source) so a student who copies the prompt
and adds four words is credited with four words, not a whole essay: yes.
The feedback the student actually received, in the teacher's or the model's
own words as the student read it: not yet. What you can see instead is the
structured half only, the checklist outcome and directive follow-through, not
the delivered sentences.

There is no rubric here either, on purpose. A rubric answers "why this score,"
which is PowerGrader's question and belongs there. This tool answers "how is
this writer developing," and per-criterion checklist outcomes already carry
that signal without several hundred words of grading-descriptor prose eating
the context window before you have read a word the student wrote.

Extended constructed responses -- the kind PowerGrader scores against a full
rubric with a Writing Timeline attached -- can be held here too, as an
ordinary dated piece alongside the short daily reps, but unscored: a
checklist written for a one-sentence rep cannot honestly grade a 400-1500
word essay, so a long piece's total, possible, and per-criterion outcomes all
come back null rather than a number that would misrepresent what was
checked. student_word_count and the flag-derived observations
(no_student_text, exceeds_word_cap) still apply, because those come from
segmentation, not from a checklist.

A long piece is truncated the same as any other quoted text when you ask for
it: with include_text=true, its raw_text is cut at max_text_chars and the
rest is dropped from what comes back, not just hidden. student_word_count is
never truncated, so it is what tells you how much of the piece you did not
just see -- a piece reporting 900 student words against a raw_text you only
received the first 2000 characters of is missing most of itself. Raise
max_text_chars deliberately when you actually need to read an extended
piece rather than a rep; the default is sized for short daily writing, not
an essay.

A piece's purpose, narrative versus argument versus explanation and so on, is
not a separate stored field yet. Infer it from the checklist prompt text if
you need it, and say when you are inferring rather than reading a fact.


How work enters the record

There is no automatic feed and no per-assignment flag to turn on. A teacher
points CanvasExpert at one Canvas assignment (from the CanvasExpert web UI,
or the equivalent CLI command) and every typed student response on that
assignment enters the record, pseudonymously, unscored, the same day. That
one action is the opt-in -- there is nothing else to configure, and nothing
happens to an assignment nobody has pointed CanvasExpert at.

This only reaches typed (online text entry) submissions read from Canvas
Expert's own local mirror -- never a live Canvas call. A DOCX upload or a
handwritten photo does not enter the record this way yet. Re-running the
same assignment is safe: it replaces that assignment's rep and each
student's submission rather than duplicating them, so pointing CanvasExpert
at an assignment again after a due-date change or a new late submission
does not create a second copy of anyone's work.


Privacy

Pseudonym-first the same way every other student-data tool here is: real
names, Canvas IDs, and SIS IDs never leave the machine. The record is private
to the teacher's own workspace and does not exist in Canvas, in the AI
Authoring folder, or anywhere outbound except through this one gated read.
Quoted spans, when you ask for them with include_text=true, pass through the
same outbound safety scan every other tool here does before they reach you.
